Ginkgo Biloba: Circulation, Memory, and Safety

Ginkgo biloba is a tree whose leaf extracts have been studied for circulation and cognition. The evidence depends on the preparation and the population studied. Results from a standardized leaf extract are not necessarily relevant to powdered leaf or an ingredient included in a multi-plant proprietary blend.

Claims about memory and brain health are common in ginkgo marketing. However, study results have been mixed, and evidence about one condition cannot be used to promise better memory in healthy consumers. There is no basis for saying that the ginkgo named on the Pineal Guardian X page removes fluoride or repairs the pineal gland.

Safety deserves attention. The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health notes that ginkgo may increase bleeding risk in people taking anticoagulant medicines such as warfarin. Ginkgo can also interact with other drugs. Fresh ginkgo seeds and crude plant material can be toxic, which is another reason to distinguish a standardized extract from an unspecified product ingredient.

People taking blood thinners, antiplatelet medicines, seizure medicines, or other prescription drugs should consult a healthcare professional before using a ginkgo-containing supplement. Anyone with planned surgery should also disclose supplement use to the care team.
