Lion's Mane: Yamabushitake and Cognitive Research

Lion's mane, also known by the Japanese name yamabushitake, is an edible mushroom studied for compounds that may influence nerve-related signaling in laboratory models. It has attracted interest in cognitive research, but human studies remain relatively small and short. Results from laboratory or animal work cannot be treated as proof of a memory benefit in people.

A 2023 pilot study in 41 healthy young adults reported some task-specific findings after lion's mane supplementation, but the authors described the evidence as tentative and called for larger studies. A 2025 randomized study in 18 healthy younger adults found no significant overall improvement in composite measures of global cognition or mood. These studies do not establish the effects of the lion's mane used in Pineal Guardian X.

Product preparations can differ in whether they use fruiting body, mycelium, powder, or concentrated extract. The amounts of bioactive compounds can vary. Without the exact form and dose in the product, even a study of lion's mane generally may not be directly relevant to this particular formula.

Lion's mane is a food mushroom, but long-term supplement safety data are limited. Some users in small studies have reported digestive discomfort, nausea, or skin reactions. Anyone with a mushroom allergy, chronic condition, or medication regimen should seek appropriate advice before taking a concentrated supplement.
