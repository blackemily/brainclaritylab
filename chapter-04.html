Circadian Rhythms and the Body Clock

Circadian rhythms are daily cycles in physiology and behavior. The best-known is the sleep-wake rhythm, but circadian timing also influences body temperature, hormone patterns, alertness, digestion, and metabolic activity. These rhythms are coordinated by a central clock in the brain and by clocks in tissues throughout the body.

The central clock receives light information from the eyes. It helps synchronize the body's internal timing with the outside world. Regular timing of waking, meals, activity, and sleep can reinforce daily rhythms. Irregular schedules, shift work, travel across time zones, and exposure to bright light late at night can make alignment more difficult.

People sometimes describe circadian disruption as a problem with the pineal gland. The pineal gland is part of the timing pathway, but it is not the only component. A circadian rhythm problem can arise from behavior, work schedules, light exposure, neurological conditions, medications, or other health factors.

A supplement marketed for the pineal gland should not be presented as a replacement for practical circadian habits or medical treatment for a diagnosed sleep disorder. Understanding daily timing is useful whether or not a person chooses to use Pineal Guardian X.
