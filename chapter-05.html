Light, Screens, and the Sleep-Wake Cycle

Light is the strongest environmental signal for setting the body's central circadian clock. Bright light in the morning generally helps signal daytime, while darkness in the evening allows melatonin to rise at the appropriate time. The timing, intensity, and duration of light exposure all matter.

Phones, tablets, televisions, and bright indoor lighting can expose people to light late in the evening. The impact depends on brightness, distance, duration, timing, and individual sensitivity. It is usually more useful to consider the overall pattern of evening light and sleep routine than to focus on one device or one wavelength as the sole cause of poor sleep.

Practical steps may include keeping a consistent wake time, getting outdoor light early in the day, dimming indoor lights before bedtime, and making the sleeping space dark and comfortable. These approaches do not require a supplement and can support healthy circadian timing.

People with persistent insomnia, unusual sleepiness, loud snoring, gasping during sleep, or major changes in sleep timing should seek appropriate evaluation. A pineal supplement should not delay investigation of sleep apnea, medication effects, restless legs, depression, or other potentially treatable causes.
