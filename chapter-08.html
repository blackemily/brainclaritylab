Reviewing the Pineal Guardian X Sales Claims

The Pineal Guardian X sales page describes the product as a brain-detox formula and states that it flushes fluoride from the pineal gland, restores natural melatonin production, and improves or restores memory. It also makes references to reversing memory loss and early Alzheimer's disease. These statements are claims made by the seller, not findings established by the product information reviewed for this guide.

To substantiate a health claim, the evidence must match the actual claim. Research showing that the pineal gland produces melatonin does not prove that this supplement increases melatonin. Research about one plant ingredient does not establish that the finished blend removes fluoride. A study of an ingredient in a different dose or preparation does not automatically validate the product's formula.

The FTC's health-products guidance says that express and implied health claims in marketing must be truthful, not misleading, and supported by competent and reliable scientific evidence. It also explains that books or other educational materials can be treated as promotional material when a marketer creates or uses them to sell a particular product. That is why the product links and the book's claims need to be handled carefully.

This guide does not claim that Pineal Guardian X detoxifies the brain, removes fluoride, reverses calcification, raises melatonin, prevents dementia, or treats Alzheimer's disease. Readers should ask the seller for published product-specific clinical evidence before relying on any such claim.
